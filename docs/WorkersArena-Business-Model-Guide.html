Section 1 — The Existing Business Model

1.1 What the platform is

WorkersArena is a two-sided local-services marketplace for home and commercial work — plumbers, electricians, cleaners, AC technicians, carpenters, painters and more. It has a third, business-facing side: companies that buy advertising. The product is bilingual (Arabic RTL / English LTR), prices everything in US dollars, and currently serves Beirut, Lebanon as its city. Profiles, quotes, bookings, reviews, lead offers and invoices all exist in both languages.

The commercial thesis is deliberately layered: workers pay to be seen (subscription), workers pay to compete (credits → leads), the platform takes a cut of work won (take rate, refunded partly by lead rebates), trust is sold (verification, featured slots, emergency markers), and companies pay to reach the audience (advertising). Customers — the demand side — are intentionally free, because charging bookers early would throttle the network effect that makes every other stream worth anything.

The four participants

ParticipantWhat they doWhat they pay
Worker (supply) Builds a profile with prices, certificates, hours and availability; buys leads; sends quotes; takes bookings; gets paid through the platform's ledger. Subscription ($7.50–$299/mo depending on plan × trade multiplier), lead credits ($1 each), paid verification ($9/$19), featured ($49/category/mo), emergency marker ($9/mo), plus the take rate on completed jobs.
Customer (demand) Searches, browses, favourites, reviews, requests jobs, pays deposit and balance. Nothing to the platform — the job price is paid to the worker; the platform's fee comes out of the worker's side.
Company (B2B) Runs advertising campaigns across 8 ad formats with city/category targeting and ROI analytics. Campaign budgets — priced at ~$10 CPM / $1 CPC in the current model.
Admin (platform) Confirms manual payments, approves payouts, edits every price and rule, reviews lead refunds, sends WhatsApp outreach, reconciles money. Collects every stream listed below.

Annual billing = pay 9 months, get 12 (25% off). New plans/prices are edited at /admin/revenue-settings, never hard-coded.
  • Category-adjusted pricing. Trades are classified by average job value: low-value (cleaning, gardening, pest control) ×0.5 → Starter $7.50; mid-value (plumbing, electrical, carpentry) ×1.0 → $15; high-value (HVAC, satellite, mechanic) ×1.5 → $22.50. The same price × multiplier is used on the instructions page, the invoice and the subscription record.
  • Trial policy. Starter/Growth get 30 days free, Pro 14 days, Business is assisted by default. Eligibility is checked server-side, once per worker; a lapsed trial never reopens.
  • The paywall (visibility gating). An expired subscription removes the worker from public search. Search visibility is workers' #1 KPI, so expiry doubles as the highest-converting upsell trigger, reinforced by 7/3/1-day renewal reminders (cron + dashboard banner) and an "expiring" status.
  • Invoicing. Every confirmed renewal mints an INV-* invoice with the exact charged amount.

1 credit = $1 by convention. Bonus credits are promotional and itemised separately. Balance is derived from an append-only WorkerCreditEntry ledger — never stored — so every grant, spend and refund adjustment is auditable.

E. Lead rebate — buying leads lowers the take rate you pay

The loyalty loop When a bought lead converts to a completed job, the lead's cost is credited back against the platform fee: rebate = min(fee × pctBps/10000, lead cost, ceiling) — default 100% of the fee share, no ceiling. The stamped fee is never rewritten; the rebate is recorded in an append-only LeadRebate row. 
Worked example (default rules)
  Quote                        = $300.00      Growth worker → 7% take rate
  Platform fee (stamped)       = $21.00       7% × 300, inside the $5–$300 band
  Gold lead the worker BOUGHT  = $20.00       (20 credits spent earlier)
  Rebate                       = $20.00       min(21 × 100%, 20, ∞)
  ─────────────────────────────────────
  Platform keeps               = $1.00        effective take rate on this job ≈ 0.33%
  Worker nets                  = $299.00      ($300 − $21 fee + $20 rebate)

The rebate is the mechanism that answers the classic worker objection — "I paid for a lead and you take a cut". Both costs are real, but only one is returned when the lead actually wins work.

G. Referral program — supply growth as a credit cost

H. Company advertising — the dormant B2B engine

  • 8 ad types: banner, slider, featured card, sponsored search, sponsored category, popup, native, video.
  • 6 placements: search results top, category page, worker-profile sidebar, sticky mobile banner (24h-dismissible), weekly email digest (with tracking pixel), retargeting overlay (fires after 2+ views or a <30s bounce).
  • Pricing model as built: impressions burn $0.01 and clicks burn $1 — i.e. roughly $10 CPM + $1 CPC. Targeting by city and category; rotation; impression/click/CTR/CPC/ROAS analytics.

1.4 How money is actually collected: the OMT / Whish manual rail

Payment automation (Stripe and MENA gateways) is intentionally deferred. Everything that costs money runs on Lebanon's two cash networks with no gateway keys and no webhook — the platform mints a signed instructions page, the buyer pays offline with a reference, and an admin confirms receipt. It is the manual twin of a provider webhook, and it currently carries all eight flows: subscription renewals, credit purchases, verification, featured, emergency, advertising campaigns, booking deposits and booking balances.

  • Tamper-proof without a webhook: each provider signs with its own salt and reference prefix (OMT- / WHISH-); the instructions page verifies the signature through the same contract the webhook route uses.
  • Idempotent per method: re-clicking the same method returns the already-minted URL; switching method re-mints, so a stale Stripe pre-mint can never leak a simulate URL to a Whish click.
  • Refunds follow the payer: a cancel resolves the provider that took the money — an OMT-paid deposit refunds via the OMT provider, not a default.
  • Reconciliation: every row lands in the admin ledger and exports as CSV (status, reference, timestamps, invoice, leg). A cron cancels unpaid subscription renewals older than 7 days and records the lifecycle event; workers can cancel their own pending renewal immediately.
  • Keyless mode: with no Stripe keys, dev/tests use a simulated provider that completes instantly; in production the registry refuses simulation, so the card path stays closed until real keys exist.

1.5 The rule that makes the take rate real money

Collection invariant The ledger never credits more than the platform has collected, and the fee is only ever taken out of money actually received. One pure function — settlementFor(facts) — is the single decision point, called by both database adapters, the customer's pay card, the worker's banner, the payout guard and the admin reconciliation. No surface reads raw payment rows to decide whether a worker can be paid.

A booking's value arrives on two legs: the deposit before the work (Booking.paymentId) and the settlement — the outstanding balance after it (Booking.settlementPaymentId). The admin's queue tags each row with a leg (deposit | settlement) so an operator can never confirm the wrong amount for the right booking.

Where a job's money can stand, and what the worker's ledger receives.
StateMeaningCredited to worker
fundedCollected ≥ quote (deposit covered the job)collected − fee — the classic path
part-fundedDeposit landed, balance didn'tCollected part now; the rest as an ADJUSTMENT when the balance is confirmed
awaiting-customerNothing collected, nothing requested yetNothing
awaiting-confirmationA manual OMT/Whish reference is issued but unpaidNothing — a reference is a promise, not money
outside-platformThe parties settled in cash on the doorstepNothing — the fee becomes a claim recoverable tranche-by-tranche from the worker's credit balance
overpaidCollected > quote (double-paid deposit)collected − fee, excess flagged for refund
no-quoteQuote-less acceptNothing (there is no job value)

Why this matters commercially: before this rule existed, a quote-only job credited a payout the platform never collected, and an approved payout paid the worker out of platform funds. Every other revenue lever — leads, credits, subscriptions, ads — sits on top of this one, so collection integrity is the foundation of the whole model.

Section 2 — Step-by-Step: How to Use It

Four journeys, each written as the literal click-path a person follows, with what the system does at every step. Every payment step is the same OMT/Whish loop shown in §1.4 — the reference changes, the mechanism never does.

2.1 Journey A — the worker, from signup to payout

  1. Register and pick a language. The worker signs up (AR/EN) and lands on the dashboard. A free trial is applied automatically server-side: 30 days on Starter/Growth, 14 on Pro, Business assisted. Eligibility is once per worker.
  2. Build the profile. Category, city, area, prices, working hours, certificates, portfolio photos (before/after), languages. Profile quality directly feeds the matching engine (trade 30% + area 12% + city 8%).
  3. Choose a plan before the trial ends. The public pricing page shows category-adjusted prices — a cleaner sees $7.50 Starter, an HVAC technician $22.50. The renew dialog quotes monthly vs annual (9-for-12) and labels the change as upgrade / downgrade / unchanged with the price difference previewed.
  4. Pay the subscription (OMT/Whish). Select method → signed /payments/manual page opens with the reference → pay at the OMT agent or in the Whish app → the admin's pending-payments card confirms → subscription activates, invoice mints, visibility is (re)granted. Until this confirms, an expired plan means the worker is absent from public search.
  5. (Optional) Buy trust products. The dashboard upgrade dialog sells verification ($9/$19), the featured slot ($49/category/mo) and the emergency marker ($9/mo) inline, each on the same rail; confirmation flips verified / featured / emergency.
  6. Buy credits. Lead Credits card → "Buy More" → package ($10/$25/$50/$100 with bonuses) → OMT/Whish → confirmation grants the credits to the append-only ledger. Balance is always derived from entries.
  7. Watch the lead board. Qualified customer requests arrive as graded offers (Bronze 5 → Emergency 35 credits), matched by weighted score, exclusive, expiring on a timer, priced by smart pricing with the reason shown.
  8. Unlock a lead. "Unlock for N credits" debits the ledger idempotently per offer and reveals contact details per the plan-tier policy. Rate the lead 1–5 afterwards — that rating moves future per-grade prices (0.8×–1.2×) and matching weights. A bad lead can be refunded once via the admin review queue.
  9. Receive booking requests and respond with a quote. The respond dialog previews "Quote $80 · Platform fee $5.60 · You receive $74.40" live — the same pure function that stamps the immutable fee snapshot on accept.
  10. Set the deposit. Accept with an optional deposit → the booking goes PENDING_PAYMENT → the customer gets a checkout (OMT/Whish instructions) → admin confirms → CONFIRMED. The fee was already stamped; times can move later, money cannot.
  11. Do the work, complete the job. On completion the settlement engine reads both legs: if the platform collected ≥ quote the ledger is credited collected − fee as an EARNING (one per booking, idempotent). If only the deposit landed, the worker sees "waiting for the customer — $X outstanding".
  12. Balance and rebate. The customer's pay card releases the outstanding balance on the same rails; when it confirms, the difference posts as an ADJUSTMENT. If the lead was bought, the rebate posts against the fee — the platform keeps fee − rebate.
  13. Read the statements. The ROI dashboard shows leads bought, quotes sent, jobs won, GMV, platform fees, subscription cost, total marketplace spend and the resulting multiple for the month. The earnings statement shows completed jobs, GMV, fees, rebates and net earnings per job.
  14. Withdraw. Payouts card → available vs pending balance → request an amount (capped at available − pending; the request reserves the amount) → admin approves or rejects → balance debits only on approval. The worker is notified either way.
  15. Refer and repeat. Share the unique referral code: +25 credits when the invitee completes their first booking, +10 for them at signup, 10 per month. Renewal reminders (7/3/1 day) and the expiring banner keep the subscription alive.

2.2 Journey B — the customer

  1. Search or browse. Category/city/keyword search (Arabic input works — the index handles it), worker profiles with rating, reviews, response rate, availability and verification badges. Featured workers sit on the homepage.
  2. Pick a slot and request. Open the worker's live availability and request a booking — or arrive through a shared ?book=…&slot=… entry link from a WhatsApp thread, which opens the dialog pre-filled (each unusable part is dropped with a reason, never a hard failure).
  3. Receive the quote. The worker responds with a quote and optional deposit. The row is transparent: "$80 · Includes platform fee $5.60 · Worker receives $74.40".
  4. Pay the deposit. Choose OMT or Whish → instructions page with reference → pay offline → admin confirms → booking flips CONFIRMED and a WA-YYYY-NNNNN invoice is minted for signed-in customers.
  5. Job runs. Either party can reschedule. Cancellations follow the 24h policy window: customer/system cancels always refund; a worker cancelling inside 24h of start keeps the deposit.
  6. Pay the balance after completion. If the deposit didn't cover the quote, a pay card names the exact outstanding amount on the same rails — paying it is what releases the worker's payout, and the UI says so.
  7. Review. At completion the platform asks for a review (ask now, remind once after 3 days, silent after 30 or once a review exists). Only customers with a completed booking can review — verifiedPurchase is computed from the reviewer's own bookings.
  8. Follow-up. Favourites, weekly digest email with sponsored section, and a request path for customers without a worker match — which is what becomes a graded lead on the marketplace.

2.3 Journey C — the company / advertiser

  1. Open the company dashboard and start a campaign builder: bilingual name, one of 8 ad types, placement, budget, date range, and city/category targeting.
  2. Pay to activate. Creation mints a PENDING invoice and payment. "Pay now" issues the OMT/Whish instructions; the campaign stays PENDING and serves nothing until the admin confirms — then it flips ACTIVE and the invoice is paid.
  3. Ads serve by placement: sponsored search top, category banner, profile sidebar, sticky mobile banner, weekly digest (pixel-measured), retargeting overlay. Clicks route through /api/ads/:id/click.
  4. Spend accrues at ~$0.01/impression and $1/click against the budget until it is exhausted or the end date passes → ENDED.
  5. Measure ROI in the analytics view: impressions, clicks, CTR, CPC, CPM, ROAS, placement breakdown, daily performance, budget utilisation, export.
  6. Refund path: admin refund → campaign ENDED, payment REFUNDED, invoice VOID (credit note), company notified with the reason.

2.4 Journey D — the administrator

  1. Confirm payments. The pending-payments card lists every OMT/Whish row with ID, scope, bilingual label, amount, method, reference, created time, status and — for bookings — the leg (deposit/settlement). Verify the buyer's reference, click Confirm (idempotent; non-admins get unauthorized), capability activates.
  2. Run pricing from revenue settings. /admin/revenue-settings edits plan prices, lead quotas, category multipliers, credit packages, grade prices, matching weights, contact-reveal policy, rebate rules (on/off, share, ceiling), fee ladder and promotions — each publish creates an audit version. Nothing above is hard-coded.
  3. Operate the lead market. The lead-market panel shows offers, credits, rebates, ratings, the smart-price/surge suggestion (one click prefills the emergency base-price adjustment when the 30-day surge verdict says healthy or overpriced), and per-grade WhatsApp templates an admin sends manually to matched workers via a pre-filled wa.me link.
  4. Review lead refunds. One request per lead, evidence reviewed, approve full/partial credit return with a note — appended to the ledger, never rewriting the spend.
  5. Approve payouts. The pending-payouts card: worker, amount, time → Approve (PROCESSED, debits balance) or Reject (nothing moves). The decision is a compare-and-swap, so two admins cannot both decide.
  6. Reconcile. The settlement reconciliation card shows fee stamped vs fee collected, customers being awaited, unbacked credit (must read $0.00), credit blocked on collection, and outside-platform claims — worst-first. Export the OMT/Whish ledger as CSV for accounting.
  7. Watch retention. The retention panel and /api/admin/retention derive trial-to-paid conversion, churn, upgrades/downgrades, average lifetime, renewal revenue/LTV and WhatsApp outreach counts from the append-only SubscriptionEvent ledger — plus at-risk workers ordered by soonest expiry for individual WhatsApp outreach.
  8. Watch quality and surge. Lead-quality analytics (weekly rating trends, per-grade stats, conversion, price-multiplier impact) and the 30-day emergency surge report with a deterministic verdict and CSV decision record; the summary also rides the weekly admin WhatsApp digest.
The one loop to remember Every purchase in all four journeys is: buyer action → PENDING payment + signed reference → offline payment → admin confirmation → capability activates → invoice + ledger row. Automation replaces only the last two steps — that is precisely what connecting Stripe (deferred, see §4) would do.

3.5 Getting paid — the payout lifecycle

  EARNING / ADJUSTMENT ──▶ POSTED ─────────────────────────────┐
       (created at completion + collection, idempotent)        │
                                                              ▼
   available = Σ POSTED earnings/adjustments − Σ PROCESSED withdrawals
                                                              │
  worker: requestPayout(amount)  ── amount ≤ available − pending
        │                                                     │
        ▼                                                     │
  WITHDRAWAL · PENDING   ← reserves the amount (no double-spend) │
        │                                                     │
   admin: decidePayout (CAS — one decision only)              │
        ├── approve ──▶ PROCESSED  ← now counts as a debit ────┘
        └── reject  ──▶ REJECTED   (dead, never counts; nothing moves)
  • Balance is derived, never stored. The ledger is the single source of truth; each row keeps only its own balanceAfter as an audit trail.
  • The payout guard is one function (payoutGuard) used everywhere — and because every posted earning is funded by construction, an unfunded legacy credit can never be paid out.
  • Approval ≠ transfer, today. PROCESSED currently means "admin approved"; wiring an actual provider payout (OMT transfer / Stripe Payouts) into the same seam is a §4 recommendation — the ledger model will not need to change.

4.5 Risk, compliance & model guardrails

  • VAT / tax on fees and subscriptions. Platform fees and subscriptions are tax events in most MENA markets (KSA 15%, UAE 5%). Price displays are USD and tax-naive today — decide explicitly whether tax is inclusive before expanding countries, and store the tax component on invoices from the start.
  • Money-holding rules for escrow. Recommendation #2 must be implemented through a licensed facilitator or the gateway's own escrow, never the platform's own account — otherwise a trust feature becomes a regulatory liability.
  • Refund exposure. Keep the platform fee refundable with a cancelled booking (current behaviour) — or charge only at completion — so a cancellation wave never turns into customer backlash. Watch the 24h window's effect on worker income and adjust with data, not anecdotes.
  • Cash-dominant payment culture. Cash on delivery is normal in the region; if cash jobs bypass the platform entirely, the take rate misses most transactions. Recommendation #4 is the counterweight — make paying through the platform visibly safer and cheap enough to be the default.
  • Supply-side price sensitivity. Informal workers are cost-sensitive: keep Starter cheap, let the rebate and volume justify the take rate, and always market "jobs, not just views". Never raise the take rate before booking volume demonstrably rises.
  • Two-sided cold start. Continue not taxing the demand side. Every proposed fee belongs on supply or companies until network effects are established.
  • Single-currency assumption. USD-only is fine for Lebanon; a second tenant currency must not leak into ledgers — mixed-currency workers are a documented concern for the multi-country wave, not a bug to discover late.
