## Phase 1 — collectable revenue, pricing clarity and lead-quality protection (approved)

- ✅ **Collectable take rate — the money first ([booking-settlement.md](booking-settlement.md)):** the take rate was *accrued*, not *collected* — `creditEarnings` credited `quote − platformFee` to the worker's **withdrawable balance** without checking whether the customer had paid, and because `Booking.paymentId` is `@unique` there was no second payment path for a quote-only job, so an approved payout paid the worker from platform funds for money the platform never received. A pure settlement engine (`src/lib/data/booking-settlement.ts`) now decides one question — *what has actually been collected for this job?* — and both adapters credit only from it: the ledger never credits more than was collected, and the fee is taken only out of money received. A **second payment leg** (`Booking.settlementPaymentId`, migration `20260922160000_booking_settlement`, `BookingStatus.SETTLED`) collects a finished job's balance on the same OMT/Whish rails as the deposit (the pending-payments card now tags `leg` so the balance can't be confused with the deposit), the customer gets a **pay-the-balance card**, the worker gets a *waiting on the customer* banner plus an honest **“we settled this directly”** declaration when the parties paid in cash (the platform's fee then becomes a claim recovered from credit balances, never an accrual), a **payout guard** states the withdrawal rule once, and the admin gets a **reconciliation card** — fee stamped vs. fee collected, money blocked on collection, outside-platform claims, and an **unbacked-credit canary** that must read $0.00. Deliberately **no gateway**, per the Phase-1 decision: this makes the existing manual rails collectable; Stripe stays deferred. Also fixed the production seed, which was itself creating the unfunded accrual (it now funds every completed demo job, and heals rows seeded before the fix); live-DB coverage in `npm run db:smoke` asserts no job in the window holds a ledger credit the platform never collected.
- ✅ **Business reduced take rate:** Business now pays 4% on completed quoted jobs instead of receiving a full exemption. Existing fee snapshots are unchanged; only new accepts use the new rule.
- ✅ **Differentiated trials:** Starter/Growth receive 30 days, Pro receives 14 days, and Business is assisted by default. Trial eligibility is server-side and once per worker.
- ✅ **Transparent credit economics:** base credits are priced at $1 each; bonus credits are displayed separately and are not silently treated as cash value.
- ✅ **Lead refund workflow:** workers can submit one request per purchased lead for invalid contact, duplicate, wrong category/area, not requested, or unreachable leads. Admins approve/reject/partially approve; approvals append credit adjustments and preserve the original spend.
- ⏸️ **Payment automation:** Stripe and other gateways remain deliberately deferred.

## Phase 2 — demand-first growth ([demand-growth.md](demand-growth.md)) (started)

Phase 1 made the money collectable. Phase 2 goes after the scarce side of the marketplace — **customers** — because the audit found the supply side monetized from every angle (subscriptions, take rate, leads, credits, upgrades, advertising) while demand had no reason to prefer the platform over asking a neighbour.

- ✅ **Instant booking:** a worker can opt in to selling a published fixed price with no request/response round-trip. The decision is a pure five-condition engine (`src/lib/data/instant-book.ts`: opt-in + `ServiceItem.fixedPrice` + per-`job` unit + an `AVAILABLE` slot + a 120-minute minimum lead time), re-run by the server action against **live rows** so a stale tab can never sell a slot the worker has blocked or a price they have changed. The deposit equals the price, so the job is **funded before it starts** — which is what the Phase-1 settlement engine credits from. Customer UI: an emerald `Book instantly — $price` primary action on the details step with the plain request button kept beside it, the checkout handed straight back (an instant booking that stalls on "pay later" is not instant), and a confirmation that says *booked*, not *requested*. Repeat cadences and emergency claims deliberately stay on the request path — a contract needs the worker's accept, and a surge on an unpublished price is not a fixed price.
- ✅ **Fixed-price packages (the shelf the opt-in needs):** the opt-in is consent, not a price — before this, `fixedPrice` was only ever true on seeded rows, so the feature was live for nobody real. Workers now publish a buy-now price on their own per-job services from the availability panel (`publishFixedPricePackageAction` + the pure `publishablePackage`, refusals `unknown-service` / `hourly` / `invalid-price`). Withdrawing is not un-pricing (the service returns to quoted pricing at its last published price), hourly rows show no input at all, and both writes are worker-scoped so a crafted name cannot price someone else's service.
- ✅ **Price benchmarks per category:** "typical quote for this trade: $X–Y" on the worker profile, built from **completed** jobs with a real quote inside a 180-day window (`src/lib/data/price-benchmarks.ts`). Percentiles (p25 / p50 / p75) rather than a mean — one 3× emergency call-out must not move a "typical price" — rounded to a $5 step so the band reads as an observation, not a quote. Below 5 samples a category reports **nothing** (two jobs is not a benchmark, and a two-row band is trivially gameable); `priceStanding` gives exactly three verdicts (below / within / above). Nothing is stored, so a benchmark can never go stale against the jobs it describes.
- ✅ **One place where major and minor units meet:** `ServiceItem.price` is stored in MAJOR units while a booking quote is MINOR. Instant booking is the only code path where both appear, so the conversion is a named constant (`MINOR_UNITS_PER_MAJOR`) with the schema comment corrected to match reality — the trap that would otherwise charge a customer a penny per dollar.
- 🔜 **Phase 2 next (in plan order):** guest → account claim (a phone-keyed guest booking becomes an account, so the second purchase has a home); a WhatsApp booking entry (start a booking from the channel customers already use); SEO expansion (city × category landing pages from the catalog already in hand); and review solicitation at completion, when the customer is happiest. Payment automation stays deferred by decision.

## Lead-marketplace phase — demand-priced leads and operating controls (started)

- ✅ **Emergency/demand lead pricing:** new offers apply the existing smart-pricing engine on top of the grade and feedback multiplier. Emergency dispatch receives a bounded 1.5× factor before the global 0.7×–2.0× clamp; rush hour, weekend, seasonal, holiday, and local supply/demand factors remain visible in the locked offer reason.
- ✅ **Immutable pricing audit:** each `LeadOffer` stores the combined multiplier and reason used when its credit price was locked. Later policy or demand changes never re-price an existing offer.
- ✅ **Admin refund decisions:** the lead-quality queue now supports full or partial credit returns, admin notes, evidence review, bilingual labels, and error feedback. Approved refunds remain append-only credit adjustments and cannot be submitted twice.
- ✅ **Subscription migration controls:** renewal/plan-change surfaces now preview upgrade vs downgrade cost, use the current category-adjusted admin catalog, and keep manual OMT/Whish checkout amounts and invoices consistent across demo and Prisma paths.
- ✅ **Surge measurement tooling:** a 30-day emergency-surge evaluation report (pure engine `src/lib/data/surge-report.ts`, admin card on Revenue Settings, CSV export at `/api/admin/revenue/surge-report`) tracks emergency purchase conversion, the base/premium credit split net of refunds, weekly trend, the gold-grade baseline, and a deterministic verdict (healthy / watch / overpriced / quality-risk / insufficient-data) with a 20-offer / 10-purchase honesty floor.
- 🔜 **Phase 2 next:** let the surge report accumulate 30 days of data, then tune the 1.5× factor (or expand surge windows) using its verdict — re-export the CSV monthly as the decision record. The weekly admin digest (`/api/cron/admin-digest`, Mondays 08:00 UTC; WhatsApp via `ADMIN_WHATSAPP_NUMBERS`, HTML email via `ADMIN_EMAILS`) now carries the surge verdict and tuning line plus the week's operational triage — failed WhatsApp deliveries (24h window, dead letters, error text), the pending lead-refund review queue, and at-risk renewals (≤30-day expiries) — so the window and the follow-ups stay visible without opening the admin. The lead-market panel also offers a one-click emergency-price prefill when the verdict is healthy/overpriced. For review before real data accumulates, `npm run db:seed-surge` backfills a deterministic 30-day emergency/gold cohort (marked with `surge-seed@workersarena.test`, idempotent) so the card can be judged populated. Add cohort-based upgrade prompts after the measurement window. Stripe, automatic billing, and payment automation remain deferred.

## Retention phase — operating visibility (started)

- ✅ **Subscription risk snapshot:** the admin retention panel now derives active, expired, 30-day expiring, retention, churn, and at-risk worker ordering from the current worker subscription rows rather than using a fixed expiry list. The clock is injectable for deterministic tests and the same adapter-fed rows work in demo and production.
- ✅ **Subscription lifecycle ledger:** trial starts, manual renewals, admin plan changes, cancellations, and future expiry/cron events use an append-only `SubscriptionEvent` ledger with demo and Prisma adapters. The admin retention panel now aggregates the available six-month cohorts and worker-level trial conversion from those events.
- ✅ **Renewal-risk outreach:** admins can send a WhatsApp-only renewal message from each at-risk worker row. The action is admin-gated, uses the configured WhatsApp provider, preserves the worker's locale, and does not fan out email/SMS.
- ✅ **WhatsApp delivery ledger:** every automated WhatsApp send lands in the `WhatsAppDelivery` ledger (pure engine `src/lib/data/whatsapp-deliveries.ts`, dual-adapter store, migration `20260920120000_whatsapp_deliveries`). Meta status webhooks (`/api/webhooks/whatsapp`, HMAC-verified) advance rows sent → delivered → read / failed; failures auto-retry at 5 min then 30 min (max 3 attempts, `/api/cron/whatsapp-retries`), and admins can re-send from the bilingual audit card on `/admin`. Dispatch results capture Meta's `wamid` so webhook events join the exact message.
- ✅ **Expiry event capture:** the subscription reminder cron records one append-only `expired` lifecycle event per worker when an expired reminder is processed. Repeated cron runs are deduplicated so cohort churn is not inflated.
- ✅ **Real-mode expiry processing:** the reminder cron now scans Prisma subscriptions, claims the 7/3/1-day and expired windows with a database compare-and-swap, updates expired rows to `EXPIRED`, records one lifecycle event, and dispatches the worker notification. This prevents duplicate reminders across concurrent cron instances.
- ✅ **Admin exports and reconciliation:** retention cohorts/at-risk workers can be exported as bilingual-safe CSV, and the full OMT/Whish manual-payment ledger (pending, paid, refunded, cancelled) has an admin-only reconciliation JSON/CSV endpoint linked from the revenue dashboard.
- ✅ **Authenticated renewal ownership:** pending renewal cancellation now resolves the worker profile from the authenticated user in real mode and rejects payments belonging to another worker; the demo adapter retains its single-worker fixture behavior.
- ✅ **Lifecycle retention analytics:** trial-to-paid conversion, churn, upgrades/downgrades, average lifetime, renewal revenue/LTV, and WhatsApp outreach sent/failed metrics now derive from `SubscriptionEvent` in both adapters and are exposed in the admin panel and retention export.
- ✅ **PWA production hardening:** production push now refuses ephemeral VAPID keys, `npm run check:vapid` gates the required secret triplet, and the production device matrix covers phones, iPads, desktop layouts, installability, service-worker updates, offline fallback, and push-capable browser paths.
- 🟡 **Cohort history and LTV:** historical lifetime/LTV and transition totals remain intentionally blank until enough production event history exists. Subscription cancellation events, stale-renewal cleanup, and settled manual-payment exports are now implemented; Stripe and automated payment collection remain deferred.

## 2. Workflow enhancements — make the journeys better

Improvements to how customers, workers, and admins *move through* the product. Ordered by leverage.

### 2.1 Discovery phase (customer picks on better signals)

- [x] **Response rate + "Free this week" on search cards & profiles** — ✅ shipped (W1). `responseRate` (from `computeResponseRate` / shared `responseRateFromCounts`) and `availableThisWeek` (from `hasFreeSlotsThisWeek`, a 7-day AVAILABLE-slot window) are stamped on the `Worker` type by both adapters — demo via `withDemoSignals` in `repo.ts`, prisma via the batched `stampWorkerSignals` in `prisma-repo.ts` — and rendered as chips on `WorkerCard` + the `ProfileHero` meta row (i18n keys `worker.responseRate` / `worker.freeThisWeek` EN+AR). Unit tests + a `db:smoke` section assert the stamps against the live DB. *Why: customers select on responsiveness and availability, not just rating.*
- [x] **Price benchmarks per category — ✅ shipped in Phase 2 (benchmarks half)** ([demand-growth.md](demand-growth.md)) — the interquartile band (p25 → p50 → p75) of **completed** jobs with a real quote inside a 180-day window, rounded to a $5 step, rendered on the worker profile with a three-way standing (below / within / above). Below `BENCHMARK_MIN_SAMPLE` (5) the category reports nothing rather than a band it cannot stand behind, and the figure is derived on read (both adapters feed the same pure engine) so it can never go stale. *Why: anchors expectations and cuts quote-shock declines at the response phase.* **Still open:** the *city* dimension (the same band scoped per city once a city has the sample size for it) and the search-page variant.
- [x] **Instant booking + fixed-price packages — ✅ shipped in Phase 2** ([demand-growth.md](demand-growth.md)) — see the Phase 2 section above: the pure five-condition engine, the worker's package editor, and the funded-up-front sale. *Why: the customer who has already decided to buy should not have to wait for a quote.*
- [x] **Emergency/demand lead price lock — ✅ shipped in Phase 2** — `computeSmartPricing` is now part of qualified-lead distribution; the resulting multiplier and reason are persisted on each `LeadOffer` alongside its locked credit price. *Why: monetize urgency without repricing workers after they see an offer.*
- [x] **Search upgrade — ✅ shipped** — production search now combines ILIKE substring with **pg_trgm Arabic fuzzy matching** (migration `20260920210000_search_trigram`: `wa_norm()` normalization twin + expression GIN indexes; candidates via `word_similarity`, ranked by the demo engine's exported `scoreWorkerQuery` so both adapters order identically — `docs/SEARCH.md`). **Geo/radius**: `nearLat`/`nearLng`/`radiusKm` filters with a distance-aware relevance boost, device-anchored when geolocation is granted, city-anchored otherwise, exposed as a "Within" select on `/search`. **Per-category conversion**: the lead funnel per trade (offered → purchased → job won, deduped by rebate attribution or converted ratings) computed purely in `category-conversion.ts` and rendered on the admin lead-quality page. *Why: Arabic typo-tolerant discovery, distance-honest results, and the §2.1 conversion data that tells pricing where demand actually is.*
- [x] **Verified-purchase-weighted reviews — ✅ shipped with the moderation queue (§3.2)** — the pure engine's `weightedRating` now ranks on a Bayesian mean over APPROVED reviews only, weighting a verified purchase ×1.5, recency (180-day half-life) and helpful votes, with a 5-review prior at 4.2 so a single 5★ can't outrank a 200-review profile; `rankingScore` adds a log-scaled volume lift and `effectiveStatus` keeps pre-moderation rows visible. The displayed stars stay the lifetime running average (what a reader can verify next to the count), while the weighted figure is the ranking input. Also: a NEW review still submits as unverified — `verifiedPurchase` is set from the booking, not the form, and is a triage signal until §3.2's queue plumbing sets it from a completed job. Medium.

### 2.2 Request phase (fix the structural weakness)

- [x] **Multi-candidate "request quotes from up to 3 workers" — ✅ shipped per [multi-candidate-quotes.md](multi-candidate-quotes.md)** — a customer posts ONE job and invites up to `MAX_QUOTE_WORKERS` (3) workers to quote it; each responds with a bid (the existing quote/deposit inputs); the customer picks a winner + a slot from the winner's availability. A new `QuoteRequest` job container (`QR-YYYY-NNNNN`, `QuoteStatus` OPEN/QUOTING/SELECTED/EXPIRED/CANCELLED, `QUOTE_SLA_MS` 48h expiry) groups the invited workers, who ARE its slot-less `Booking` rows (`QUOTING`/`QUOTED` — `Booking.startAt/endAt` became nullable, migration `20260814080745_multi_candidate_quotes`). The winner's pick reuses the EXACT atomic `AVAILABLE → RESERVED` CAS + overlap guard (the winner becomes a normal slot-bound REQUESTED booking and flows through the existing respond pipeline; losers are system-DECLINED — slot-less, nothing to free; the job flips to SELECTED once). Both adapters (demo + `prisma.$transaction`) implement the same rules; the requests cron also expires quote jobs (`/api/cron/requests` → `quotesExpired`). UI: a "Get quotes" dialog on every worker profile (pick up to 3 from the profile + related pool), a **Quote requests** tab on `/bookings` (one card per job: bids per worker, "Accept this quote" → the winner's slot picker → `selectQuoteAction`), and the worker dashboard Requests tab renders QUOTING invites as an inline bid form + QUOTED as "Awaiting the customer's decision". i18n `booking.quotes*` + `booking.status.quoting/quoted` EN+AR; unit tests in `tests/bookings.test.ts` (create/duplicate/over-limit, bid, pick + guards, SLA expiry, actions round-trip), mapper tests in `tests/prisma-repo.test.ts`, and a `db:smoke` W2 section (create → 2 bids → pick → loser DECLINED + slot claimed → respond → CONFIRMED · backdated expiry → EXPIRED + bids DECLINED, idempotent). The customer-side list lookup matches by the signed-in `customerId` as well as email/phone — the quote form's email is optional, so a customer who skips it still sees their own jobs (both adapters + the /bookings page; unit-tested in `tests/bookings.test.ts` and asserted in the smoke's quote section with the email stripped). **The single highest-leverage workflow change — converts a lottery into a marketplace.** Large (but reused the Booking rails — no new payments logic).
- [x] **Request SLA (nudge + auto-expire) — ✅ shipped** — a `REQUESTED` booking that sits unanswered is dead air for both sides. The `runRequestSlaEngine` cron (`GET /api/cron/requests`, CRON_SECRET-gated like the other cron jobs) nudges the worker once past `BOOKING_SLA_NUDGE_HOURS` (24h) — `worker-request-nudge` notification, idempotent via the `Booking.lastSlaNudgeAt` CAS stamp (migration `20260813130000_request_sla`) — and auto-cancels past `BOOKING_SLA_EXPIRE_HOURS` (48h): status → CANCELLED (`cancelledBy` system), the slot frees back to AVAILABLE (rule 3), a SYSTEM audit event is appended, the customer gets `customer-request-expired` (deep-link `/bookings`), and `BOOKING_CANCELLED` is logged to the activity feed so the funnel's cancelled bucket and Recent activity tell one story. Both adapters return the same `RequestSlaRun` shape (demo dedupes nudges per process; prisma stamps the column). The two windows are tunable constants shared by both adapters. i18n `notifications.types.bookingRequestNudge/Expired` EN+AR; unit tests in `tests/request-sla.test.ts` (pure window boundaries + demo lifecycle: nudge-only, expire frees the slot + notifies, mixed batch, idempotent re-run) and a `db:smoke` W2 section (backdated request → nudge + auto-expire → slot freed → both notifications → feed entry → re-run expires/nudges nothing, with self-heal + cleanup). ALL THREE sides now render the same live ticking clock as a `BookingSlaCountdown` (compact on the rows): the admin dispute view's banner, the customer /bookings rows and the worker dashboard cards each show the real minutes ticking down against `requestSlaExpiryMs` plus the urgency bar (>50% green, 20–50% amber, <20% red pulse) — the worker's copy swaps to the nudge variant once the cron nudged. The quote cards' closing window now drains with the SAME shared `SlaUrgencyBar` (extracted from the countdown so the two deadlines can never drift): a live ticking "Closes in Nh Nm" line against the real `QUOTE_SLA_MS` deadline the cron enforces. Everything time-derived is made hydration-safe by the shared `useSsrSafeNow` hook: each server page passes its own render-time clock down as a `nowSeed` prop and the components render from it until mount (then the countdowns tick via `tick: true` — including the quote card's closing window), so the SSR markup and the client's first render are identical — no "A tree hydrated but…" mismatch on any of the three pages. The e2e hydration-smoke matrix (`tests/e2e-smoke.test.ts`) now guards the whole surface: `/admin/bookings/BK-1001` (the deep link whose banner used to mismatch on every load), `/dashboard`, `/bookings` (customer rows) and `/search`, EN + AR, asserting a zero-error browser console — and, beyond the console guard, CONTENT-checks the compact SLA countdown itself (`expectSlaBar`): the localized "Request auto-expiry" progressbar must render with a numeric `aria-valuenow` plus the ticking countdown copy on `/dashboard` and `/bookings`, so a silent regression that stops the countdown from rendering fails the matrix even with a clean console. *Why: less dead air, fewer stale slots — the worker is reminded while there's still time, and the customer gets a definitive answer instead of silence.*
- [ ] **💡 Guest phone OTP** — guests are phone-keyed with no verification; a one-time OTP raises trust enough that workers stop declining "anonymous" requests, and it de-risks M3 deposits for guests. Medium.

### 2.3 Response & negotiation phase

- [x] **Customer ⇄ worker chat thread — ✅ shipped** — a per-booking negotiation thread keyed on `Booking.id` (the `BookingMessage` model, migration `20260815090000_booking_chat`), so quotes are no longer one-shot and negotiation happens **inside the booking's record** instead of WhatsApp. The sender is actor-stamped like an audit entry (`senderRole` customer/worker/admin + optional real user id), and **quote sharing** lets the worker attach a price to a message (minor units, rendered as an in-thread quote chip). Both adapters implement the same rules: `getBookingMessages` (oldest-first read) and `sendBookingMessage` (append, unknown-booking → null) via `getBookingById` — the permission-gated `sendBookingMessageAction` (the same ownership gate as the audit-email action: the booking's customer, its worker, or a platform admin; guests can't send) validates text + the optional quote (major → minor ×100) and revalidates all three surfaces. One **shared `BookingChat` component** renders the identical thread on all three sides — the customer `/bookings` row, the worker dashboard booking rows (with the quote-sharing composer) and the **admin dispute view** (read-only, so the audit trail carries the whole conversation) — with a **WhatsApp deep-link fallback** (`buildWhatsappChatLink` in booking-ui.ts: `wa.me/<digits>?text=<prefilled booking context>`, targeting the other party's number — the worker's WhatsApp for the customer, the customer's phone for the worker). i18n `booking.chat*` EN+AR; unit tests `tests/booking-chat.test.ts` (demo adapter: ordering, actor stamping, quote share, unknown-booking) + `tests/booking-chat-action.test.ts` (permission gate, validation, quote conversion) + `tests/booking-chat.test.tsx` (thread rendering, quote chip, WhatsApp link, composer per role, EN+AR) + the `toDomainBookingMessage` mapper test; `db:smoke` exercises the thread on a live booking. ### 2.5 Privacy-preserving communication — ✅ shipped

- [x] **Masked phone numbers for worker-customer communication** — ✅ shipped. Both workers and customers see only platform-provided temporary numbers (e.g., +1-800-555-0101) throughout the booking lifecycle. Real phone numbers are never displayed in the UI. The `CallButton` component initiates privacy-protected calls through the masked number system. See [PRIVACY-COMMUNICATION-FLOW.md](PRIVACY-COMMUNICATION-FLOW.md) for full details.

- [x] **Emergency bypass with immediate masked calling** — ✅ shipped. For 24/7 emergency services, masked numbers are created immediately on request submission. Workers receive urgent notifications with masked numbers ready for immediate contact. The emergency toggle is available in the booking dialog for workers with the emergency badge.

- [x] **SMS fallback for emergency notifications** — ✅ shipped. When push notifications fail for emergency bookings, the system automatically falls back to SMS. Emergency SMS bypasses the `NOTIFY_SMS_ENABLED` config — safety takes priority. Bilingual templates (EN/AR) with urgent action-oriented messaging.

- [x] **Admin emergency dashboard** — ✅ shipped. Real-time monitoring of emergency requests with summary cards, response time metrics, privacy status, and auto-refresh. Available at `/admin/emergency` for admin-only access.

- [x] **Contact details release on arrival/completion** — ✅ shipped. Real contact details are only released when the booking reaches `inProgress` (worker arrives) or `completionPending`/`completed` (job done). The `contactDetailsReleasedAt` timestamp tracks when details are released.
